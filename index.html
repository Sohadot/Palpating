01 / THESIS

Digital health learned to measure.
Palpating asks what it means to feel.

The body does not only produce values that fit neatly into a dashboard. It also has a physical state: stiffness, elasticity, pressure, tension, texture, mobility, deformation and resistance.

Clinical palpation has long used touch to interpret parts of that state. Emerging sensing, haptic and imaging systems can make some of those physical properties measurable, transmissible and computationally interpretable.

The opportunity is not to digitize a hand. It is to make physical state legible to computation.

04 / COMPUTATIONAL LEGIBILITY

A physical sign becomes useful to computation through a chain of translation.

  1. 01Sense

    A physical interaction or property is captured.

  2. 02Represent

    The observation becomes structured data.

  3. 03Transmit

    Meaning survives movement across devices or distance.

  4. 04Compare

    Observations can be related across time, location or context.

  5. 05Interpret

    Humans or computational systems can reason over the representation.

This is an explanatory chain, not a clinical workflow or certification protocol.

Its purpose is narrower and more defensible: to name and organize an emerging interface between physical bodily state and computation.
